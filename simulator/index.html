Laid out as the device's own six-pack and read in the units it prints: knots, feet, feet per minute, degrees per second. The sliders feed a simulated L76K that emits real NMEA and the production parser decodes it, so the telemetry below is the firmware's answer and not the knob's. The ball sits under the needle, as it does in a turn coordinator. Turn is what a bank holds: 3 °/s is a standard rate turn, 13 to 18 a glider thermalling, and past 30 the alarm stops believing the projection, which is why the slider goes to 60. With GYRO off in the six-pack menu the needle is the GNSS track differentiated once a second, which the receiver reports to a hundredth of a degree; switch the gyro on and the needle answers in milliseconds instead. Slip is the rudder: it feeds a simulated BHI260AP whose firmware the device uploads at boot, so the ball takes a few seconds to appear and then lags the slider the way a damped ball in a tube does.

Traffic (real ADS-L / ALP-TAS frames)

4 NM
Each aircraft is encoded as a real frame of its own system and decoded by the firmware's receive path: ADS-L scrambled + CRC'd, ALP-TAS encrypted the way the 2024 protocol does. Both are Manchester bursts on the same channels, framed by the one sync window the dwell arms.

Every burst on the air, heard or not, with the frame decoded the way a receiver decodes it. A burst outside the dwell the firmware armed reads DEAF: that is a slot-map bug, visible. The upper channel's dwell runs 800..1200, so the strip's head is its tail and we still listen there. Between the dashed lines, 450..1000, is where our own bursts go.
